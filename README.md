# Peer Support in Campus Anti-Scam Initiatives — Research Instruments

This repository contains the research instruments used in a study of a
university campus anti-scam ambassador programme, which trains students
(house tutors, student leaders in residential colleges, and volunteers)
to support their peers in preventing and responding to scams.

The study examines the programme from both sides of the peer-support
relationship:

- **Students**: their scam experiences, awareness of and contact with the
  anti-scam ambassadors, help-seeking behaviour in scam incidents, and
  overall views of the programme.
- **Anti-scam ambassadors**: their role and preparation, routine
  prevention activities, the support they provide to students, and their
  own support needs.

All materials are shared to support transparency and replication. Author
and institution information is withheld to preserve double-blind review
anonymity.

## Repository Contents

| File | Description |
| --- | --- |
| `Student_Anti-Scam_Survey.md` | Survey questionnaire for students (32 questions across five parts: scam experiences, awareness of and contact with ambassadors, support in specific scam incidents, overall views, and background information). |
| `Anti-Scam_Ambassador_Survey.md` | Survey questionnaire for current and former anti-scam ambassadors (31 questions across six parts: role and preparation, routine prevention activities, providing support to students, support needs, personal experiences and self-assessment, and background information). |
| `student_interview_guide.md` | Semi-structured interview guide for students (21 questions covering awareness of the ambassadors, scam-related help-seeking, views on support, and suggestions). |
| `ambassador_interview_guide.md` | Semi-structured interview guide for anti-scam ambassadors (20 questions covering involvement and preparation, day-to-day work, conversations with students, communication and collaboration, and suggestions). |

## Instrument Design Notes

- **Social support framework.** Both surveys frame help around five
  categories of social support — instrumental, informational, emotional,
  esteem, and network support — allowing the support students expect and
  receive (and the support ambassadors feel able to provide) to be
  compared directly.
- **Conditional display logic.** Survey questions annotated with
  `display if ...` were shown only to respondents meeting the stated
  condition (e.g., those who had received scam messages or had sought
  help from an ambassador).
- **Peer-support focus.** The student instruments distinguish between
  everyday contact with ambassadors (information sharing, outreach) and
  support sought during specific scam incidents, so that peer support
  can be analysed as both a preventive communication channel and a
  help-seeking resource.
- **Programme evaluation.** Ambassador instruments cover training,
  guidelines, role boundaries, and support needs, providing input for
  improving peer-delivered anti-scam programmes.

## Anonymization

One question in each survey asks whether the respondent is a local
student; the institution's name is redacted (`[anonymized for review]`).
No participant or author identifying information appears in any file.
