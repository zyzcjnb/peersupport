# Ambassador Interview Guide

## Introduction

Thank you for taking part in this interview. We are interested in your
experiences as a campus anti-scam ambassador, including how you became
involved, the work you carry out, your interactions with students, and
your experiences when responding to students' concerns. There are no
right or wrong answers. Please answer based on your own experiences and
views.

## 1. Involvement and Preparation

1.  Could you tell me about your involvement in the anti-scam ambassador
    programme? How did you first become involved?

2.  How did you learn about the responsibilities and expectations of the
    role when you first joined?

3.  What preparation, if any, did you receive before starting the role?
    Could you describe what it involved? How, if at all, did this
    preparation shape the way you approached the work? If you did not
    receive any preparation, how did you learn what to do?

## 2. Day-to-Day Work and Contact with Students

4.  Could you describe what you typically do as part of the programme?
    If possible, please draw on a recent example.

5.  Have you had opportunities to interact with students through this
    work? If so, could you describe one interaction? How did it begin,
    and what happened?

6.  If you have shared anti-scam information or organized related
    activities, how did you decide what to include? What information or
    materials, if any, did you draw on?

## 3. Conversations with Students and Individual Support

7.  As part of your role, have you had conversations with students about
    scam-related questions or experiences? If so, how did these
    conversations usually begin?

If so, please describe one occasion in detail, starting from when the
situation first arose.

8.  What happened in that situation? What led you to become involved?

9.  How did you understand the concern the student raised? What
    information did you draw on in forming that understanding?

10. What did you do next? How did you decide how to respond?

11. Did you discuss the situation with anyone or seek advice? If so,
    what led you to do so, and what advice or assistance did you
    receive? If not, how did you decide how to proceed?

12. What happened after your conversation with the student? Were you
    involved in any further steps? If so, what did they involve?

13. Was there anything about the situation that you found uncertain or
    difficult to handle? If so, what made it difficult, and how did you
    respond?

If you have not had conversations with students about scam-related
questions or experiences, you may describe another aspect of your work
that involved contact with students, if applicable.

## 4. Communication and Collaboration

14. As part of your role, have you had occasions when you communicated
    or worked with other people? If so, could you describe one such
    occasion?

15. What led to that communication or collaboration? What did the people
    involved do, and what happened afterwards?

16. Have you exchanged work-related experiences or information with
    other ambassadors? If so, when do these exchanges tend to take
    place, and what do you discuss?

## 5. Reflections and Suggestions

17. Looking back on your involvement in the programme, how would you
    describe your overall experience? Were there aspects of the work
    that went smoothly or that you found difficult? Please explain.

18. What information, resources, or support, if any, have you been able
    to draw on in carrying out the role? How, if at all, have they
    shaped your work? Is there anything else that would have been useful
    to you?

19. What changes, if any, would you suggest for the programme in the
    future? What makes you suggest these changes?

20. Is there anything else about your experience as an anti-scam
    ambassador that you would like to add?
